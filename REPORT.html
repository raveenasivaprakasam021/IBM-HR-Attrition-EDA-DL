2. ABSTRACT

Voluntary employee attrition introduces substantial operational friction, intellectual capital depreciation, and severe financial replacement costs within enterprise ecosystems. This academic study presents an end-to-end exploratory data visualization and deep learning diagnostic framework using the canonical IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition Dataset (N = 1,470 instances, 35 attributes). Rigorously implementing Ben Fry's 7 Stages of Data Visualization, we execute moment diagnostics, Tukey's interquartile range outlier fencing, non-parametric Mann-Whitney U testing, and categorical Chi-Square tests of independence. To advance beyond classical linear modeling, a 3-layer deep feed-forward Multi-Layer Perceptron (PyTorch MLP) with Batch Normalization and Dropout regularization was engineered, hyperparameter-tuned across three architectural variants, and benchmarked against regularized Logistic Regression. The deep architecture achieved 84.16% test accuracy, an ROC-AUC of 0.8284, and an out-of-sample sensitivity (recall) of 66.67%, outperforming the linear baseline by +22.23% in capturing true flight-risk personnel. Permutation feature importance identified mandatory overtime, job involvement, and promotion stagnation as primary turnover drivers.

3. INTRODUCTION & OBJECTIVES

3.1 Economics of Voluntary Workforce Attrition: In knowledge-intensive enterprises, human capital forms the fundamental competitive moat. Replacing a departed specialized employee incurs direct overhead between 50% and 200% of their annualized base salary (Cascio & Boudreau, 2016). These costs comprise recruitment agency commissions, talent acquisition interview hours, signing bonuses, and a 6-to-9 month productivity ramp-up latency.

3.2 Technical Objectives: (1) Clean and diagnose structural integrity; (2) Characterize distributional moments and Tukey's IQR fences; (3) Perform non-parametric hypothesis testing (Mann-Whitney U and Chi-Square); (4) Implement Ben Fry's 7 Stages across 6 diverse visual representations; (5) Formulate and tune a Deep MLP in PyTorch; (6) Compute model-agnostic Permutation Feature Importance; and (7) Quantify precision-recall trade-offs against a linear baseline.

6. EXPLORATORY DATA VISUALIZATIONS (BEN FRY'S 7 STAGES + 6 FIGURES)

Figure 1: Target Class Distribution & Imbalance Ratio (5.2 : 1)
  • What it shows: Dual-panel visual presenting raw headcount bar frequencies (1,233 Retained vs. 237 Departed) and donut chart showing the 83.9% vs. 16.1% proportion.
  • Why it was created: Demonstrates severe target imbalance to justify why raw accuracy is misleading, requiring sensitivity (recall) and ROC-AUC evaluations.
  • Insights obtained: A naive classifier guessing "No" achieves 83.88% accuracy while missing 100% of at-risk staff. Imbalance handling is paramount.
Figure 2: Continuous Probability Density Estimation & Moment Diagnostics
  • What it shows: KDE distribution plots for MonthlyIncome, Age, and TotalWorkingYears with Mean, Median, Skewness, and Kurtosis markers.
  • Why it was created: Diagnoses non-normality and justifies non-parametric hypothesis tests and robust feature standardization.
  • Insights obtained: MonthlyIncome displays heavy positive skewness (γ1 = +1.37) and leptokurtic peakedness (γ2 = +1.00), showing large executive salary disparity.
Figure 3: Non-Parametric Bivariate Comparison Across Attrition Classes
  • What it shows: Side-by-side Tukey boxplots for MonthlyIncome, TotalWorkingYears, and YearsAtCompany segmented by Attrition with two-tailed Mann-Whitney U test p-values.
  • Why it was created: Statistically validates whether medians differ significantly across classes without Gaussian assumptions.
  • Insights obtained: Resigning employees earn a median monthly compensation of $3,202 vs. $5,204 for retained staff (U = 100,620.5, p = 2.95e-14). Early tenure is the primary turnover risk window.
Figure 4: Categorical Diagnostics with Contingency Chi-Square Significance
  • What it shows: Stacked percentage bar chart of OverTime vs Attrition and horizontal turnover rate across 9 Job Roles with Chi-Square statistics.
  • Why it was created: Uncovers critical role and workload vulnerabilities and validates dependency via Chi-Square contingency testing.
  • Insights obtained: Mandatory OverTime is the single strongest categorical predictor (Chi2 = 87.56, p = 8.16e-21), with 30.5% attrition for overtime staff vs. 10.4% for non-overtime. Sales Reps face 39.8% turnover.
Figure 5: Triangular Lower-Bound Pearson Correlation Heatmap
  • What it shows: Lower-triangular correlation matrix across 18 continuous and ordinal features alongside the numeric Attrition target.
  • Why it was created: Detects multicollinearity clusters and linear bivariate associations with employee turnover.
  • Insights obtained: Strong collinearity exists between JobLevel and MonthlyIncome (r = +0.95), and TotalWorkingYears and JobLevel (r = +0.78). Direct correlations with attrition are negative with career tenure.
Figure 6: Multivariate High-Dimensional Feature Space Pairwise Interactions
  • What it shows: Pairwise scatter interaction projections and diagonal KDE contours across Age, MonthlyIncome, TotalWorkingYears, and YearsAtCompany segmented by Attrition.
  • Why it was created: Visualizes non-linear decision boundaries and high-dimensional clustering dynamics.
  • Insights obtained: Dense attrition clustering is localized in early-career stages (Age < 32, MonthlyIncome < $4,000), illustrating that turnover is non-linearly concentrated in junior staff.
